How TypeQuest teaches (the method behind it)
  • Home row first, one key set at a time. Every lesson adds a small set of keys and nothing else until those feel automatic.
  • Accuracy before speed. Each lesson needs 92% accuracy to pass. Fast typing built on errors has to be unlearned later.
  • Eyes on the screen. The on-screen keyboard lights up the next key and its finger, so you stop looking down.
  • Short sessions beat marathons. Typing is a motor skill like an instrument. 10 to 15 minutes a day beats one long weekly session.
  • Your misses shape your practice. Every keystroke is tracked. The weak-key drill serves you the exact keys you keep missing.
  • Real words early. Drills use real words and sentences as soon as possible, because that is what your hands will actually type.
  • Play counts as practice. The arcade games drill the same patterns as the lessons, with scoring and streaks to keep you coming back.
